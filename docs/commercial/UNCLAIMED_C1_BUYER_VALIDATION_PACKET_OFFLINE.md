## 4. Buyer hypotheses — NOT CANONICAL

The following are buyer hypotheses to test, not validated customer segments and not a ranking.

### H1 — Professional unclaimed-property / asset-recovery operators

Possible job:
- screen large numbers of opportunities;
- decide where additional human research is worth spending;
- preserve evidence and reduce false-positive effort.

Key uncertainty:
- whether their existing workflows already solve targetability cheaply enough that Unclaimed adds insufficient value.

### H2 — Legal / estate / probate / recovery-service practices

Possible job:
- assess whether an unresolved asset case is sufficiently actionable before committing professional time;
- preserve provenance and escalation logic.

Key uncertainty:
- whether case volume, workflow ownership and regulatory/professional constraints support a repeatable software purchase.

### H3 — Unclaimed-property / recovery advisory teams serving organizations or estates

Possible job:
- prioritize recovery opportunities and create auditable research packages;
- coordinate evidence, compliance and case economics.

Key uncertainty:
- whether the initial beneficiary/owner-resolution product output matches their actual unit of work.

### Buyer qualification rule

A useful C1 interview target should ideally:
- already spend human time or money deciding which cases deserve further work;
- have recurring case flow rather than a one-off personal claim;
- be able to describe the current alternative;
- care about provenance/reliability;
- have a plausible decision/budget path for a bounded pilot.

Do not treat an owner/beneficiary being contacted about their own property as proof of a B2B paying buyer.

## 5. Discovery questions

The discovery conversation should learn before selling.

### Current job
1. How do you currently decide which unclaimed-asset cases deserve further research?
2. What information must be known before you invest more time or money?
3. Which parts of that decision are manual?
4. Where do cases most often become unproductive?

### Cost / pain
5. What consumes the most operator time?
6. Which mistakes or false positives are most expensive?
7. What causes a case to be abandoned?
8. Is the problem recurring enough to justify dedicated tooling?

### Current alternative
9. Which internal tools, public searches, vendors or manual workflows are used today?
10. What works well enough that you would not replace it?

### Trust / evidence
11. What evidence would you need to trust a CONTINUE/STOP recommendation?
12. What must remain explainable to a reviewer, attorney, client or compliance function?
13. Which data/privacy boundaries would prevent adoption?

### Buying path
14. Who owns this workflow operationally?
15. Who can approve a pilot or software/service purchase?
16. What would a low-risk pilot have to prove?
17. Would a sanitized targetability report be enough to evaluate the concept, or would you require a representative/real workflow?

Avoid leading questions such as "Would you pay X?" before the buyer has described the problem, current alternative and value.

## 6. Evidence classification

### C1 — Problem evidence

Record C1 only when a qualified operator/buyer provides credible evidence that:
- the job exists in their workflow;
- it is recurring/costly/risky enough to matter;
- the current alternative is identifiable;
- a decision maker or buying path is at least partially understood.

A polite reply or generic interest is not C1 by itself.

### C2 — Solution evidence

Record C2 when a qualified buyer does something costly enough to demonstrate real interest, for example:
- asks to review a project/case-specific output;
- agrees to a bounded pilot design;
- provides or authorizes representative inputs;
- schedules a substantive evaluation with the relevant operator/decision maker.

### C3 — Transaction evidence

Record C3 only for:
- paid pilot;
- purchase;
- signed LOI with economic commitment;
- fee-bearing agreement;
- equivalent strong transaction signal.

Positive feedback without economic commitment is not C3.

## 7. Pilot hypothesis

Preferred first pilot structure after C1/C2:

- bounded case/workflow count;
- fixed scope;
- explicit legal/privacy boundaries;
- no broad automation;
- clear input/output;
- measured human minutes;
- measured decision cost;
- buyer review of evidence quality and usefulness;
- explicit end-of-pilot CONTINUE / REVISE / STOP decision.

Pricing is UNKNOWN.

Do not invent a price before buyer/problem evidence exists.

Possible units to test later, not adopt now:
- paid fixed-scope pilot;
- per reviewed targetability package;
- subscription/workspace for recurring operators;
- workflow/service fee where legally and commercially appropriate.

Success-fee/recovery-linked models require separate jurisdiction/professional/legal review before adoption.

## 10. Stop / pivot rules

Do not continue building by inertia if credible buyer discovery repeatedly shows one or more of:

- targetability decision is not a material problem;
- current alternatives are already sufficiently cheap/reliable;
- buyer cannot act on the output because value evidence is required earlier;
- customer acquisition requires disproportionate manual work with no credible automation path;
- legal/privacy/professional constraints make the delivery model impractical;
- the buyer's true job is materially different from the product output.

In those cases choose CONTINUE, NARROW, PIVOT, FREEZE or STOP using evidence, not sunk technical effort.
